# Local Manager inventory review

The new inventory UI lives at `/manager2.0` and `/purchase2.0`, backed by separate SQLite review data. The original `/manager` and `/purchases` templates are preserved byte-for-byte from the verified live versions. Both versions share the existing business data and APIs; these are separate screens, not separate businesses or databases. It is independent of the business simulator. It does not connect to the live POS, send orders to a real kitchen, or change live stock.

With Python and Jinja2 installed, run `python server.py` here, then open http://127.0.0.1:4192/manager2.0?tab=inventory. The test POS is at http://127.0.0.1:4192/pos and local Purchasing is at http://127.0.0.1:4192/purchase2.0. The server binds only to loopback; this local harness is not a public deployment or an authentication service.

The initial recipe is the confirmed Crispy Chicken Loaded Fries recipe: prepared potatoes 150 g, marinated chicken breast 150 g, salad 50 g, white sauce 30 ml, Luuv special sauce 30 ml, hot sauce 30 ml, mozzarella 20 g, oil 50 ml, flour 50 g. A fresh database keeps it as a draft with zero stock. At the user's subsequent request, the review database now contains 12 sample stock items with varied quantities, example unit costs and minimum stock levels; `python sample_stock.py` recreates this setup without replacing existing nonzero counts. These are local examples, not the real opening quantities.

Stock is displayed as ingredient piles, liquid jars or packaging stacks, with a List option. The exact quantity and MOQ use each item's defined stock unit (Kg, L, nos, etc.). MOQ means **minimum stock level**, confirmed by the user; stock at or below it is highlighted in red. The MOQ chip edits that item's threshold. The full-graphic quantity is explicitly labelled: four times the configured MOQ, or 10 units / 100 nos when no MOQ is configured. This is an illustration scale, not a claimed storage capacity.

**New ingredient** is available from Stock and the recipe editor. Enter a name, a defined unit and an optional MOQ. It starts at zero quantity; creating it from a recipe also adds a row to the unsaved recipe. Quantities in nos must be whole numbers.

## Review flow

1. Manager → Recipes: edit quantities or add/remove ingredients, then save a new draft. Earlier versions remain available as read-only records.
2. Manager → Stock on hand → Audit: enter the physical count in the item's unit, add a quick note (or tap a reason), then **Save count**. The difference appears automatically, without an extra confirmation popup. Unit cost is shown only for positive opening stock that has no valuation. The correction records the before/count/difference, reason, actor and timestamp.
3. For an isolated demonstration, the local POS page has an explicit **Load sample stock** control. Its quantities and prices are test values, and its audit records say so.
4. Activate a saved draft when opening stock is ready. Create a local POS order, then send it to the kitchen. This deducts the recipe quantities once. The local server pushes a stock-change notification after the transaction commits; every connected Manager view refreshes immediately and briefly shows the quantity decrease on the affected cards. Reconnecting fetches a fresh snapshot. A five-second fallback is used while disconnected, with a periodic reconciliation when connected.
5. Create an order, change and activate the recipe, then send the earlier order: it keeps its saved recipe. New orders use the new version. Retrying a kitchen send does not deduct twice.

Local review data persists in `data/preview.sqlite3`. Automated tests use separate databases and do not alter the review database.

The top **To order / prepare** summary lists items at or below their configured MOQ, showing remaining quantity, MOQ and the shortfall to the minimum in each item's own unit. Prepared items say **Prepare in branch**; purchased ingredients remain items to order. It does not place supplier orders or invent a purchase target. **Graphs only** hides editing/audit controls and the detailed summary for a compact live display; open `/manager2.0?view=graphs` directly or use the view switcher.

## Prepared item recipes

**Recipes** contains both menu items and prepared items. The existing **Prepared salad** stock item is linked to its preparation recipe; there is no duplicate salad stock. A fresh database leaves the salad recipe empty, without inventing ingredients or quantities.

1. Select **Prepared salad**, or choose **+ Prepared item recipe** to link an existing stock item or create a new finished item.
2. Enter what the recipe makes (g for Kg stock, ml for L stock, or the item's own unit), ingredient quantities and an optional recipe note. Save a draft and activate it. Preparation recipes may be activated before ingredients arrive; stock sufficiency is checked when completing the batch.
3. After physically making it, choose **Complete batch**. The recipe amount scales the ingredient quantities. Enter the measured finished yield; a differing yield requires a note. Completing the batch atomically deducts the immediate ingredients and adds finished stock, carrying ingredient cost into the finished item's weighted average value.
4. Add the prepared item to a menu recipe using its recipe unit. The Loaded Fries recipe already uses **Prepared salad 50 g**. Selling it consumes salad only; vegetables were consumed when the batch was made. Prepared components can also be ingredients in another preparation recipe, without recursively consuming their original raw materials.
5. **Completed batches** retains the recipe version, ingredient and output snapshots, before/after quantities, author, time and note. Later recipe edits cannot change these records. Duplicate submission adds no second batch; stale active versions, insufficient stock, self/circular recipes and fractional nos are rejected. Live stock views update after the batch commits.

The user requested a sample salad recipe for testing. `sample_preparation.py` created **LOCAL-RECIPE-0003**: sample cabbage 650 g + carrot 250 g + onion 100 g → 1,000 g salad. The recipe note and raw ingredient names explicitly identify these as samples, not the confirmed Kurunegala recipe. **LOCAL-BATCH-0001** added 1 Kg to the existing 0.35 Kg salad balance. **LOCAL-ORDER-0010** then consumed 50 g, leaving 1.30 Kg. The three raw vegetable quantities stayed unchanged during the sale. All nine previous orders and the Loaded Fries recipe were preserved. Evidence is in `data/sample-preparation-verification.json`; the script will not rerun when this report exists. Review data was backed up before the schema upgrade.

At the user's explicit request, `verify-review-orders.cjs` created three additional local orders (`LOCAL-ORDER-0004`–`0006`, quantities 1/2/1) using the user's active recipe version `LOCAL-RECIPE-0002`, including their added box at 1 nos per portion. All ten deductions matched, three pre-existing orders were preserved, and a duplicate send deducted nothing. Evidence is in `data/sample-orders-verification.json`. This script intentionally creates review orders, unlike the isolated automated checks, so do not rerun it merely to inspect the report.

## Purchasing through the existing app

The new `/purchase2.0` page extends the existing bill/expense UI with a branch-stock panel. `/manager2.0` extends Manager with **More → Inventory & recipes**, plus direct entry at `/manager2.0?tab=inventory`. The original routes keep their original UI. Native header, navigation and approval cards are retained. Only inventory and purchase-bill actions are connected in this local harness; unrelated accounting, payment, expense, upload and administration actions are outside this preview.

1. Manager → **Open Purchasing** passes the active branch, never local stock payloads or sample IDs to another origin. The purchase page reads the latest low-stock list from `inventory_purchasing.restock`.
2. Prepared salad is labelled **Prepare in branch** and excluded from purchased-item checkboxes. Quantities use Item stock units. The suggested gap subtracts pending stock bills awaiting approval. Reaching MOQ exactly remains red; the purchaser chooses actual purchase quantities.
3. Choose an existing stock-enabled category, select the items for one vendor and enter received quantities. **Add selected to bill** uses the existing category-card bill form. Existing vendor, receipt, note, date, item quantities and prices are preserved. Duplicate item selections keep the bill's current line. The category is explicitly selected rather than silently reclassifying packaging.
4. Enter the actual unit prices and vendor, then **Send for approval**. A pending Purchase Bill does not increase stock. Both app views show pending amounts/quantities.
5. Open **Manager approvals**, check the bill lines and approve or reject. Approval posts stock once; rejection adds nothing. The local adapter uses distinct Local purchaser / Local manager roles and blocks self-approval. Each referring app screen selects its indicated preview role, with a loopback-only cookie fallback for direct API checks; this is not production authentication. Production keeps its existing Frappe session and role checks.
6. Manager views refresh after a committed receipt. In production the existing `purchases._decide_purchase_bill → inventory.receive_purchase_stock` remains the only receipt writer; Manager reads that warehouse's native Bin balances. No second receive-stock action or payment is added.

The earlier standalone request/partial-receipt prototype (`purchasing.py`, `purchases.html` and its SQLite tables) is retained for history and regression comparison but is no longer served by `/purchases`. It is not the production integration contract. Low-stock planning does not send a supplier order. Actual goods received are recorded using the existing bill/approval flow.

## Per-order ingredient changes

The local POS has **Customize ingredients · without / replace**. Each recipe ingredient can remain **As recipe**, be **Without**, or be **Replace** with a selected stock item and an explicit quantity in that replacement's recipe unit. Changes apply to every portion in that order. Different customizations need separate orders in this one-item preview. A kitchen note is optional.

Creating the order snapshots the active recipe and the actual ingredient list, together with named changes and kitchen instructions. Sending consumes those actual ingredients only. If hot sauce is omitted, it is not deducted. Replacing hot sauce 30 ml with white sauce 30 ml adds to the existing white sauce 30 ml, so white sauce usage becomes 60 ml per portion. Existing recipe and order records are not edited. Replacement usage is checked against the branch’s current per-ingredient stock rules when the order is sent. A shortage outside its configured allowance blocks deduction atomically; an allowed shortage records the full negative balance and a warning. A stale recipe selected for customization must be reloaded. These instructions are shown on local order cards; live kitchen ticket routing is still pending integration.

Waiting orders also have **Customize order**. **Save revised order** creates a replacement using the original order's saved recipe version. The original quantities and recipe stay in history with a replacement link, and its kitchen-send action is blocked. Concurrent revisions can create only one replacement. Orders whose stock has already been deducted cannot be revised. Existing review orders are never automatically revised by an update or a test.

## Branch and ingredient POS stock rules

Manager → Inventory → **Stock rules** configures a branch default and ingredient overrides. The available rules are **Block if stock is short**, **Percentage tolerance** (0–100%), and **Allow negative · no limit**. New ingredients inherit the branch default. Saving requires Manager access, binds to the active branch, checks a revision to prevent overwriting another manager's changes, and logs before/after settings. Existing review data starts with the prior blocking behavior; no tolerance amounts or negative-stock permissions were silently enabled.

Percentage tolerance is based on the actual saved recipe usage for the entire order being sent, including portion count and omissions/substitutions. It caps the **resulting negative balance**, so previous deficits count too. Example: chicken 150 g × 10% permits a balance down to −15 g; ten portions (1,500 g) permit −150 g. It is not an allowance that resets at zero for every sale. A 1-nos ingredient with 10% tolerance still cannot go to −1 nos; 100% would permit that first −1. Unlimited negative is available explicitly when the branch wants sales to continue despite a larger recorded shortage.

A successful sale always deducts the full ingredient quantities, retains the effective rule snapshot, and records shortage warnings. Rules are evaluated at send/payment consumption time so a manager can unblock a waiting order; already posted sales and their rules remain unchanged. Duplicate sends do not repeat deductions or rewrite warnings. Negative quantities appear in red, including when MOQ is unset, and enter the reorder list. Recent shortage sales are visible in Stock rules and the local POS. Rules only apply to POS consumption; preparation batches still require their raw ingredients. Physical audit counts remain nonnegative, but can correct an existing negative system balance with a comment. Partial purchase receipts and preparation output can reduce negative balances without division-by-zero in the local valuation preview.

The Frappe implementation stores rules on Restaurant (`luuv_pos_stock_policy`), uses branch/Item/Bin locks for checking consumption, and stores the policy snapshot on the Stock Entry. A dedicated per-document StockEntry subclass applies the native ledger allowance only to a validated issue or an incoming receipt reducing negative stock. Global Stock Settings, shared Item negative flags and other branches' policies are not changed. Batch/serial tracked shortages are still rejected. Schema additions are in `manager_inventory_versions.py`.

This ERPNext adapter is prepared locally, not deployed or proven on a full Frappe test site. Before release, validate native valuation rates/accounting, backdated receipt and valuation repost behavior, and native payment/KOT requests against the installed ERPNext build. Missing valuation/configuration errors are not swallowed or converted into successful bills. The production POS recipe/customization integration limitations described above still apply.

## Separate UI routes

`hooks.py` maps `/manager2.0` to `manager_v2` and `/purchase2.0` to `purchase_v2`, keeping Python controller names importable. Their controllers reuse the original session, branch and role gates, with version-specific login return URLs. Inventory cross-links and the local POS point to version 2. The original routes are not redirected. Purchase 2.0 uses a separate local draft key so opening or clearing a bill there cannot overwrite an original Purchasing draft.

The version 2 browser pages omit the legacy PWA manifest and service-worker registration; installing or caching the legacy shell would reopen the wrong UI. Existing installed apps, their manifest endpoints, service workers and caches are unchanged. The routes are deployed on luuvgrand.com; localhost retains its separate review database.
